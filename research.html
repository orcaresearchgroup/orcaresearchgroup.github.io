Research

We study how the ocean, atmosphere, and surface waves influence one another, from short-lived wind events near the coast to eddies that shape regional ocean circulation. We combine satellite observations, in situ measurements, and coupled numerical simulations to trace how momentum and energy move between the ocean and the atmosphere.

Ocean eddies and regional circulation

We study how eddies form and separate from the Loop Current, and how interactions between ocean currents and the atmosphere shape circulation in the Gulf of Mexico.

  • Loop Current eddy detachment and separation · Ocean Science, 2026
  • Current feedback and Gulf of Mexico circulation · Journal of Physical Oceanography, 2022

Winds, waves, and coastal currents

We investigate how strong winds affect waves and currents near the coast. Our work includes gap-wind events in the Gulf of Tehuantepec and surface circulation in Todos Santos Bay.

  • Surface currents during a Tehuantepec gap-wind event · Meteorology and Atmospheric Physics, 2023
  • Surface circulation in Todos Santos Bay · Continental Shelf Research, 2024

Observing air–sea exchanges from space

We explore how satellite missions such as the proposed ODYSEA mission could observe surface winds and currents together and help us understand how energy moves between the ocean and atmosphere.

  • Assessing the proposed ODYSEA satellite mission · Remote Sensing, 2025
